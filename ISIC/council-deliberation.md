## Expert 1 — Dr. Elena Varga (Industrial Classification)

### Key Question: Is ISIC the right primary frame?

**Assessment:** ISIC Rev 4 is a reasonable but imperfect choice. The core tension is that ISIC classifies *industries* (what an establishment does), while codes of ethics are issued by *professions* (what a worker is trained to do). A doctor is a doctor whether they work in ISIC Section Q (Health), Section M (R&D), or Section O (Public Administration).

**Recommendation: Use ISIC as the primary organizational frame, but supplement with profession-aware annotations.**

Rationale:
1. **ISIC is where the user's request starts** — and it provides a globally harmonized, exhaustive partition of all economic activity. No sector is missed.
2. **ISCO (International Standard Classification of Occupations)** would be the natural complement for profession-based mapping, but ISCO has 436 unit groups at 4-digit level — far too granular for a first pass.
3. **The practical mapping works well for most regulated professions** — doctors map cleanly to Division 86, lawyers to 69, engineers to 71. The cross-cutting problem mainly affects support professions (HR, IT, accountants) who work across industries.
4. **Hybrid approach:** For each ISIC division, identify the *dominant professions* within that division and search for their codes. Record the profession name alongside the ISIC code so a future ISCO crosswalk is possible.

**NAICS concordance:** ISIC Rev 4 maps cleanly to NAICS 2022 at the sector level (both already in the vault). For US-specific searches, use NAICS terminology (e.g., "NAICS 621 Ambulatory Health Care" is well-known to US professional bodies).

**Risk:** Some professions will appear under multiple ISIC divisions. Deduplication at consolidation is essential.

## Expert 2 — Prof. Marcus Chen (Comparative Professional Ethics)

### Key Question: What counts as a "code of ethics"? Inclusion criteria?

**Assessment:** The landscape of professional ethical guidance is broader than just "codes of ethics." A typology is needed:

| Type | Definition | Binding? | Example |
|------|-----------|----------|---------|
| **Code of Ethics** | Principles-based document articulating core values and ethical obligations of a profession | Usually voluntary, sometimes licensure-linked | AMA Code of Medical Ethics |
| **Code of Conduct** | Rules-based document specifying acceptable/prohibited behaviors | Often enforceable via disciplinary action | CFA Institute Code of Conduct |
| **Ethics Guidelines** | Advisory document offering guidance on ethical issues | Non-binding, advisory | UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI |
| **Standards of Practice** | Technical-ethical hybrid specifying professional competency requirements | Often linked to licensure | NASW Standards for Social Work Practice |
| **Oaths & Pledges** | Declarative commitment to ethical principles | Symbolic, sometimes ceremonial | Hippocratic Oath, Engineer's Ring |
| **Industry Principles** | Sector-wide voluntary commitments by trade groups | Voluntary, reputational enforcement | Responsible Care (chemical industry) |

**Recommendation: Include all six types, but tag each with its type.** This gives the richest dataset. The synthesis phase can filter by type later.

**Inclusion criteria (recommended):**
1. Must be issued by an organization representing a profession, industry, or regulatory function — not an individual company
2. Must contain explicit ethical content (values, principles, obligations, prohibitions) — not purely technical standards
3. Must be publicly accessible (we need a URL)
4. Must be current or historically significant (superseded codes may be noted but flagged)

**Exclusion criteria:**
1. Individual company codes of conduct
2. Academic analyses *of* professional ethics (those belong in CEI-Lit-WS)
3. Pure safety regulations with no ethical framing (e.g., OSHA standards per se)
4. Informal ethical advice (blog posts, opinion columns)

## Expert 3 — Dr. Adaeze Okonkwo (Labor Relations)

### Key Question: Which labor organizations issue ethical codes?

**Assessment:** Labor unions and trade bodies are an under-explored source of ethical codification. Unlike professional associations (which regulate members' competence), unions and trade bodies focus on worker rights, workplace ethics, and industry responsibility. Their codes tend to emphasize:

- Fair treatment and non-discrimination
- Workplace dignity and safety (ethically framed, beyond mere compliance)
- Responsible industry practices (environmental, social)
- Solidarity and collective responsibility

**Key categories of labor/trade bodies that issue ethical codes:**

1. **Craft unions with professional standards** — e.g., actors' unions (SAG-AFTRA), musicians' unions, journalists' unions (NUJ). These often have codes addressing professional integrity.
2. **Global union federations** — IndustriALL (manufacturing), UNI Global Union (services), ITF (transport), IUF (food/agriculture), BWI (building/woodworkers), PSI (public services), Education International. Many have ethical charters or responsible industry frameworks.
3. **Industry trade associations** — PhRMA (pharmaceutical ethics), ICMM (mining principles), Responsible Care (chemicals), FSC (forestry), MSC (fisheries). These are increasingly ethics-focused, not just lobbying bodies.
4. **Employer federations** — IOE (International Organisation of Employers) has ethical guidelines; national equivalents like CBI (UK), BDA (Germany).
5. **Tripartite bodies** — ILO conventions and codes of practice, which are jointly developed by governments, employers, and workers.

**Search strategy for labor sources:**
- Search "[industry] union code of ethics" and "[industry] trade association ethical code"
- Check global union federation websites directly (there are only 10 GUFs — quick to scan)
- ILO sectoral codes of practice are a goldmine (they cover mining, construction, agriculture, health services, etc.)
- For US: AFL-CIO affiliated unions often have ethical standards embedded in constitutions

**Risk:** Union ethical codes are often embedded in constitutions or bylaws, not published as standalone documents. May need to note "ethics provisions within constitution" as a document type.

## Expert 4 — Prof. Katarina Lindström (International Governance)

### Key Question: Which countries have the richest professional ethics infrastructure?

**Assessment:** Professional ethics codification varies enormously by country and legal tradition:

**Tier 1 — Deepest infrastructure (search priority):**
| Country/Region | Strength | Notes |
|-------|----------|-------|
| **United States** | Most professions self-regulate with explicit codes | AMA, ABA, AICPA, IEEE, NASW, etc. — the most documented landscape |
| **United Kingdom** | Chartered professional bodies with royal charters | GMC, SRA, RICS, IET, BCS, CIPD — strong statutory backing |
| **Australia** | Well-documented professional standards | AHPRA (health), Engineers Australia, Law Council — accessible online |
| **Canada** | Provincial professional regulation + national bodies | Often bilingual (EN/FR), strong provincial variation |

**Tier 2 — Strong infrastructure, some access barriers:**
| Country/Region | Strength | Notes |
|-------|----------|-------|
| **EU member states** | Strong via EU-level directives + national bodies | CCBE (law), EFPA (psychology), FEANI (engineering) |
| **Japan** | Well-developed but often in Japanese only | JSME, JSCE, JMA |
| **India** | Large number of professional councils | BCI (law), MCI (medicine), ICAI (accounting) — some in English |
| **South Africa** | Post-apartheid professionalization push | HPCSA (health), ECSA (engineering), LSSA (law) |

**Tier 3 — Emerging or less accessible:**
- Latin America, Middle East, Southeast Asia — codes exist but are often harder to locate online and may be in local languages

**Recommendation:**
- For each ISIC division, search **US + UK + Australia + one global/international body** as the baseline
- Note EU-level bodies when they exist (CCBE, EFPA, etc.)
- Flag Canadian, Indian, South African, and Japanese equivalents when found
- Do NOT attempt exhaustive country-by-country coverage in this first pass — that's a Phase 2 expansion

**Key international umbrella bodies to check for every profession:**
- International Federation of [profession] (e.g., IBA for law, WMA for medicine, IFAC for accounting)
- These global federations almost always have model codes that member nations adopt

## Expert 5 — Dr. James Whitfield (Information Science)

### Key Question: What search strategies maximize recall?

**Assessment:** Professional ethics codes are a form of "grey literature" — they sit outside academic databases and are scattered across organizational websites. Standard academic search (PubMed, Scopus) will miss most of them.

**Recommended search strategy (per ISIC division):**

1. **Direct organizational website visits** — The single most reliable method. For well-known professional bodies (AMA, IEEE, ABA), go directly to their ethics/standards page.

2. **Structured web search queries:**
   - `"[profession] code of ethics" filetype:pdf` — catches downloadable codes
   - `"[profession] code of conduct" site:.org` — targets professional organizations
   - `"[professional association name] ethics"` — when you know the org name
   - `"[industry] ethical guidelines" OR "code of practice"` — catches UK/Commonwealth terminology
   - `"[industry] responsible [practice/business]"` — catches industry trade group codes

3. **Known registries and compilations:**
   - **Illinois Institute of Technology's Center for the Study of Ethics in the Professions (CSEP)** — maintains the largest collection of professional codes at `ethics.iit.edu/codes`. This is the single best starting point.
   - **OECD regulatory databases** — for professions with statutory regulation
   - **Council of Europe professional ethics resources**
   - **ILO NORMLEX** — for labor-related ethical codes and conventions

4. **Snowball from known codes** — Each code often references peer organizations. The AMA code links to WMA, ICN, etc.

5. **Wikipedia as a finding aid** — Wikipedia articles on professions often list the relevant professional body and link to their code of ethics. Not a primary source, but an efficient discovery mechanism.

**Quality control:**
- Verify each URL resolves to an actual document (not a 404 or paywall)
- Prefer primary sources (the org's own website) over third-party summaries
- Note when a URL points to a summary vs. the full code
- Record access date (grey literature URLs are unstable)

**Risk:** Some organizations have recently moved codes behind member-only logins. Note these as "restricted access" rather than omitting them.

## Expert 6 — Prof. Rosa Delgado (Legal Scholar)

### Key Question: How to distinguish statutory vs. voluntary codes?

**Assessment:** Professional ethical codes exist on a spectrum of legal enforceability:

| Category | Legal Status | Enforcement | Example |
|----------|-------------|-------------|---------|
| **Statutory/Regulatory** | Enacted by legislation or delegated to a statutory body | License revocation, fines, criminal penalties | State Bar rules (US), GMC standards (UK) |
| **Self-Regulatory (with teeth)** | Adopted by a professional body with disciplinary authority | Expulsion from profession, public censure | CFA Institute (can revoke charter) |
| **Self-Regulatory (aspirational)** | Adopted by a professional body, advisory only | Moral suasion, peer pressure | IEEE Code of Ethics |
| **Industry Voluntary** | Adopted by a trade association or consortium | Reputational, membership withdrawal | Responsible Care (chemical industry) |
| **International Model** | Promulgated by an international federation as a template | No direct enforcement — member nations may adopt | WMA Declaration of Geneva |

**Recommendation: Tag each code with its enforcement category.** This is analytically critical because:
- A statutory code tells you what society *requires* of a profession (minimum ethical floor)
- A voluntary code tells you what a profession *aspires to* (ethical ceiling)
- The gap between floor and ceiling is where the most interesting ethical content lives

**Data field to add:** `enforcement_level`: `statutory | self_regulatory_binding | self_regulatory_aspirational | voluntary | international_model`

**US-specific note:** In the US, professional regulation is primarily a state function. The ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct are "model" at the national level but become statutory when adopted by state supreme courts. Record the national model code and note state adoption.

## Expert 7 — Dr. Tomoko Hayashi (Organizational Sociology)

### Key Question: What types of organizations issue codes of ethics?

**Assessment:** The organizational landscape is more varied than commonly assumed. A taxonomy of code-issuing bodies:

| Org Type | Description | Typical Code Focus | Examples |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Professional association** | Membership body for practitioners of a specific profession | Professional competence, client duties, peer obligations | AMA, ABA, IEEE, NASW |
| **Regulatory/licensing board** | Government-chartered or statutory body that controls entry to a profession | Minimum standards, public protection, fitness to practice | State medical boards, SRA (UK), HPCSA (South Africa) |
| **Labor union** | Worker representation organization | Worker rights, workplace dignity, solidarity | NUJ, SAG-AFTRA, global union federations |
| **Industry trade group** | Membership body for companies in a sector | Industry reputation, responsible business practices, consumer trust | PhRMA, API, ICMM |
| **International federation** | Global umbrella for national professional bodies | Harmonized principles, cross-border standards | WMA, IBA, IFAC, WFOT |
| **Multi-stakeholder initiative** | Coalition of companies, NGOs, governments | Sector-wide ethical standards, supply chain ethics | Fair Labor Association, Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative |
| **Standard-setting body** | Organization that develops formal standards | Technical-ethical hybrids, "should" statements | ISO 26000, GRI Standards |
| **Accreditation body** | Organization that accredits educational programs or institutions | Ethical competency requirements, curriculum standards | ABET (engineering), AACSB (business schools) |

**Recommendation:** The search should cover at least the first five types for every ISIC division. Multi-stakeholder initiatives and standard-setting bodies should be included when they contain explicit ethical content (not purely technical).

**Structural insight:** The density of code-issuing organizations correlates with three factors:
1. **Client vulnerability** — professions serving vulnerable populations (patients, children, the elderly, criminal defendants) have the most codes
2. **Information asymmetry** — professions where practitioners know far more than clients (medicine, law, finance) codify ethics to build trust
3. **Historical incidents** — professions that experienced public ethics scandals tend to develop codes in response (accounting after Enron, journalism after phone-hacking)

**Prediction by ISIC section (expected organizational density):**

| Density | ISIC Sections | Rationale |
|---------|--------------|-----------|
| Very High (20-60+ orgs) | M, Q | Professions with licensure, client vulnerability, long history |
| High (10-30 orgs) | K, P, J (div 58-59), O | Regulated sectors with public trust obligations |
| Medium (5-15 orgs) | F, H, R, N (div 80) | Trade bodies with safety-ethics overlap |
| Low (2-8 orgs) | A, B, C, D, E, G, I, L, S | Industrial sectors — more trade group codes than professional codes |
| Minimal (0-3 orgs) | T, U | Household employment, extraterritorial — very few formal codes |

## Consensus Recommendations

### 1. Classification Framework
**Use ISIC as primary organizer, annotate with profession names.** This ensures exhaustive sector coverage while preserving the ability to build an ISCO crosswalk later.

### 2. Inclusion Criteria (agreed)
Include all six document types identified by Prof. Chen (codes of ethics, codes of conduct, ethics guidelines, standards of practice, oaths/pledges, industry principles). Tag each with its type.

### 3. Scope (agreed)
Include professional associations, regulatory bodies, labor unions, industry trade groups, and international federations. Exclude individual company codes. Include multi-stakeholder initiatives when they contain explicit ethical content.

### 4. Geographic Priority
- **Primary search:** US + UK + Australia + relevant international federation
- **Secondary:** EU-level bodies, Canada, India, South Africa, Japan
- **Flag but do not exhaustively search:** other countries (Phase 2 expansion)

### 5. Additional Data Fields
Add to the schema based on council input:
- `enforcement_level`: statutory | self_regulatory_binding | self_regulatory_aspirational | voluntary | international_model
- `document_type_detailed`: code_of_ethics | code_of_conduct | ethics_guidelines | standards_of_practice | oath_pledge | industry_principles
- `profession_name`: the specific profession (distinct from ISIC division)

### 6. Search Strategy
- Start with IIT CSEP database (ethics.iit.edu/codes) as a baseline finder
- Direct organizational website visits for known bodies
- Structured web searches with filetype:pdf and site:.org filters
- Check global union federations and ILO sectoral codes
- Snowball from references in found codes

### 7. Priority Ordering
Wave 1 (M, Q, K) → Wave 2 (P+J, O+R, F+H) → Wave 3 (A+B, C) → Wave 4 (remaining)
This front-loads the sectors with the richest ethics infrastructure.

### 8. Risk Assessment
| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Professions cross-cut ISIC divisions | Deduplicate at consolidation; annotate profession name |
| Codes behind member-only logins | Record as "restricted access" with org name and URL |
| Non-English codes missed | Focus English-language first pass; flag known non-English codes |
| URL instability (grey literature) | Record access date; prefer org-hosted primary sources |
| Conflating safety standards with ethics codes | Apply inclusion criterion: must contain explicit ethical content |
| Over-counting via document variants | One entry per code per organization (not per edition) |
