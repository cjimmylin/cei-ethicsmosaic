## Overview

Section U (Division 99) covers international organizations, diplomatic missions, and other extraterritorial bodies operating outside any single national legal framework. This section is expected to yield 5–10 ethics frameworks. The main finding is that major international organizations (UN, IMF, World Bank, UNDP, NATO, EU Commission) all have staff codes of conduct or ethics frameworks. Most of these bodies appear in Section O (Public Administration) in prior wave research for their domestic-governance functions — they are cross-listed here for their extraterritorial activities.

The ICRC (International Committee of the Red Cross / Red Crescent) also has a staff code and a broader humanitarian operations code, both relevant here.

## Division 99 — Activities of Extraterritorial Organizations and Bodies

### UN Ethics Office / United Nations
- **Document:** UN Ethics Framework ("Putting Ethics to Work: A Guide for UN Staff") + Standards of Conduct for the International Civil Service
- **URL:** https://www.un.org/en/ethics/
- **Policy portal:** https://policy.un.org/en/accountability/ethical-standards-and-integrity
- **Notes:** UN Ethics Office established in 2006. Five strategic functions: advice; protection against retaliation; financial disclosure; ethics training; coherence of ethical standards. Core values: integrity, professionalism, respect for diversity. The "Standards of Conduct for the International Civil Service" (ICSC) governs all UN system staff including values of independence, loyalty, impartiality, integrity, accountability, and respect for human rights. This framework applies system-wide across all UN agencies.
- **Cross-listing note:** UN was referenced in Section O (Public Administration) for its O84 governance functions. Cross-list here for extraterritorial application.
- **Scope:** International (all UN member states)
- **Enforcement:** UN staff regulations; Ethics Office; protection against retaliation mechanism

### UNDP — United Nations Development Programme
- **Document:** UNDP Code of Ethics (June 2020)
- **URL:** https://www.undp.org/accountability/ethics
- **PDF:** https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/undp/library/corporate/ethics/UNDP%20CODE%20OF%20ETHICS%20-%20June%202020.pdf
- **Notes:** UNDP has its own standalone Code of Ethics separate from the general UN framework. Covers UNDP-specific ethical obligations. Ethics Office functions: advice and guidance; protection against retaliation; ethics training; annual financial disclosure; coherence of standards.
- **Scope:** International
- **Enforcement:** UNDP Ethics Office; financial disclosure program; whistleblower protections

### IMF — International Monetary Fund
- **Document:** IMF Code of Conduct for Staff
- **URL:** https://www.imf.org/external/hrd/code.htm
- **Additional:** IMF Ethical Conduct and Core Values Certification: https://www.imf.org/en/About/ethics-and-integrity/ethical-conduct-and-core-values-certification-and-financial-disclosure-policy-for-imf-staff
- **Executive Board Code:** https://www.imf.org/external/hrd/edscode.htm
- **Notes:** Staff code covers all IMF employees. Also a separate Code of Conduct for Executive Board members. Annual ethics and integrity reports published publicly. Staff financial disclosure required.
- **Scope:** International (Articles of Agreement member states)
- **Enforcement:** IMF Ethics Office; annual reports; disciplinary procedures

### World Bank Group
- **Document:** World Bank Group Code of Ethics
- **URL:** https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/147281468337279671/wbg-code-of-ethics
- **PDF:** https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/147281468337279671/pdf/WBG-Code-of-Ethics.pdf
- **Board Officials Code:** https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/786861541011483695/pdf/131464-BR-PUBLIC-after-11-01-18-Code-of-Conduct-for-Board-Officials.pdf
- **Notes:** Core values: impact, integrity, respect, teamwork, innovation. Code of Ethics derived from WBG core values. Separate Code of Conduct for Board Officials. Code of Professional Ethics first distributed to all staff (milestone noted in WBG timeline 1936 equivalent; modern version substantially revised). Whistleblower protections and INT (Integrity Vice Presidency) enforcement.
- **Scope:** International
- **Enforcement:** WBG Integrity Vice Presidency (INT); Ethics Office; financial disclosure; sanctions

### NATO — North Atlantic Treaty Organization
- **Document:** NATO Code of Conduct
- **URL:** https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/hr/code-of-conduct.pdf (PDF)
- **Notes:** Agreed by North Atlantic Council December 18, 2020. Applies to all NATO civilian and military staff across all NATO bodies. Core principles: integrity, loyalty, accountability, impartiality, professionalism. Read alongside NATO Civilian Personnel Regulations and applicable national military regulations.
- **Scope:** International (29 member nations)
- **Enforcement:** NATO HR regulations; disciplinary procedures under member state authority

### European Commission — Code of Conduct for Commissioners
- **Document:** Code of Conduct for Members of the European Commission
- **URL:** https://commission.europa.eu/about/service-standards-and-principles/ethics-and-good-administration/commissioners-and-ethics/code-conduct-members-european-commission_en
- **Staff ethics:** https://commission.europa.eu/about/service-standards-and-principles/ethics-and-good-administration/staff-and-ethics_en
- **Notes:** Entered into force 2018 (updated version). Covers all Commissioners during and after tenure (post-service restrictions, cooling-off periods). EC staff are governed separately via Staff Regulations and Code of Good Administrative Behaviour. European Ombudsman oversees ethics standards for EU institutions (https://www.ombudsman.europa.eu/webpub/2024/ethics-standards/en/).
- **Scope:** European Union (international/extraterritorial)
- **Enforcement:** Independent Ethical Committee; European Court of Justice; European Ombudsman

### ICRC — International Committee of the Red Cross
- **Document 1:** Code of Conduct for Employees of the ICRC
- **URL:** https://www.icrc.org/en/document/code-conduct-employees-icrc
- **Document 2:** Code of Conduct for the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and NGOs in Disaster Relief
- **URL (IFRC):** https://www.ifrc.org/document/code-conduct-international-red-cross-and-red-crescent-movement-and-ngos-disaster-relief
- **PDF (ICRC):** https://www.icrc.org/en/download/file/271318/code_of_conduct_2023.pdf
- **Notes:** Two distinct documents. (1) ICRC employee code covers internal ethical obligations of ICRC staff (integrity, accountability, humanitarian principles). (2) The broader Red Cross/Red Crescent + NGO Code of Conduct for Disaster Relief, drawn up 1992, sets industry-wide ethical standards for humanitarian operations. The latter was signed by major international humanitarian organizations and is referenced as the foundational humanitarian sector ethics code.
- **Cross-listing note:** ICRC referenced in Section O (Public Administration, O84 defense/public order) in prior wave analysis. Cross-list here for Division 99 extraterritorial classification.
- **Scope:** International
- **Enforcement:** ICRC institutional mechanisms; humanitarian sector self-regulatory adoption

### UNESCO — United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization
- **Document:** UNESCO Ethics Office guidance / Handbook for Ethical Conduct
- **URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/ethics
- **Handbook:** https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000386038
- **Notes:** UNESCO Ethics Office governs staff conduct. The 2021 Ethics Office Annual Report noted plans to elaborate a specific UNESCO Code of Conduct complementary to the ICSC Standards. UNESCO is governed primarily by the ICSC Standards (same as UN system generally) with agency-specific guidance. Ethics Office provides advice, training, financial disclosure oversight.
- **Cross-listing note:** UNESCO was referenced in prior wave analysis (Education sector, P). Cross-list here for Division 99 extraterritorial classification.
- **Scope:** International
- **Enforcement:** UNESCO Ethics Office; ICSC Standards apply

### WTO — World Trade Organization
- **Document:** Rules of Conduct for the Dispute Settlement Understanding (DSU)
- **URL:** https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/dispu_e/rc_e.htm
- **Notes:** WTO Rules of Conduct apply to panel members, Appellate Body members, experts assisting panels, arbitrators, and WTO Secretariat staff involved in dispute settlement. The Ethics, Risk, and Compliance Office was established in the WTO Secretariat in September 2024. Staff are subject to general international civil service standards. This is a narrower ethics instrument (dispute resolution) plus emerging general ethics framework, rather than a full standalone professional code.
- **Scope:** International (164 member states)
- **Enforcement:** WTO Ethics, Risk, and Compliance Office (est. 2024); international civil service norms

## Not Found / Gaps

- **WTO staff general code (beyond dispute settlement):** WTO only recently established its Ethics Office (2024); no mature standalone public staff code found comparable to IMF/World Bank.
- **Regional development banks (ADB, AfDB, IDB):** These were not searched in depth but are analogous to World Bank and likely have similar ethics frameworks. Not included in current wave.
- **G7/G20 Secretariats:** These are rotating secretariats without permanent staff, so no institutional code applies.
- **OECD (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development):** Not searched; analogous extraterritorial body with likely ethics framework. Potentially for future wave.

## Assessment

Section U yields 8 confirmed organizations with ethics codes or frameworks. The coverage is reasonably dense given the limited number of major extraterritorial institutions. The pattern is consistent: all major intergovernmental organizations use the ICSC (International Civil Service Commission) Standards of Conduct as a baseline and layer agency-specific codes on top. The ICRC is unique in also having an operational (not just staff) code applicable to the entire humanitarian sector.

**Total confirmed entries for Section U:** 8 organizations (most are cross-listings from prior waves)
