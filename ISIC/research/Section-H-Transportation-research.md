# Section H — Transportation and Storage

**ISIC Rev 4 Divisions 49–53**
**Researcher notes:** Transportation has some of the strongest and most institutionalized ethics codes across all ISIC sectors. Aviation stands out — ALPA's Code of Ethics is among the most comprehensive union-based professional ethics codes in any sector. Maritime has both IMO/ILO international conventions (STCW, MLC 2006) and the Nautilus International / RMT Code of Conduct for the Merchant Navy. Supply chain and procurement (Div 52-53 overlap with logistics) are served by ISM, CIPS, and SCMA. Land transport is the thinnest; ATA trucking and APTA transit have governance-level codes but no strong professional ethics codes for drivers.

---

## Division 49 — Land Transport and Pipeline Transport

### ATA — AMSA Code of Ethics (Moving & Storage)
- **Organization:** American Trucking Associations (ATA) — Moving and Storage Conference
- **Document:** Code of Ethics (Moving and Storage Conference)
- **URL:** https://www.trucking.org/sites/default/files/2021-04/ATA%20Moving%20%20Storage%20Conference%20Code%20of%20Ethics.pdf
- **Year:** Current (PDF hosted 2021)
- **Key principles:** Professional moving and storage industry conduct standards.
- **Notes:** ATA's main national body does not publish a general trucking code of ethics separate from its conference division codes. The Moving & Storage Conference code is the most explicit.
- **Enforcement:** Member association requirement.
- **Scope:** US

### APTA — Governance Code of Ethics (Public Transit)
- **Organization:** American Public Transportation Association (APTA)
- **Document:** Governance Code of Ethics
- **URL:** https://www.apta.com/about/governance/board-approved-policies/governance-code-of-ethics/
- **Year:** Current (Board-approved policy)
- **Key principles:** Legal compliance; orderly meeting conduct; antitrust compliance; ethical behavior for all officers, directors, and committee chairs; zero-tolerance harassment at events.
- **Notes:** Governance-level code for APTA officers and directors, not a professional ethics code for transit workers/bus drivers. However, it is the leading formal ethics statement for the public transit industry association.
- **Enforcement:** Board-enforced for leadership.
- **Scope:** US

## Division 50 — Water Transport

### Nautilus International / RMT / UK Chamber of Shipping — Code of Conduct for the Merchant Navy
- **Organization:** Nautilus International (seafarers union), National Union of Rail, Maritime and Transport Workers (RMT), UK Chamber of Shipping
- **Document:** Code of Conduct for the Merchant Navy
- **URL:** https://www.nautilusint.org/en/news-insight/resources/partnership-publications/code-of-conduct-for-the-merchant-navy/; direct PDF: https://alphamarine.ie/assets/PDFs/Code-of-Conduct-for-the-Merchant-Navy.pdf
- **Year:** Published May 13, 2019
- **Key principles:** Disciplinary rules and procedures for seafarers employed on ships; recognizes unique demands of seafaring occupation; addresses shipboard harassment and bullying (aligned with 2016 MLC amendments).
- **Approved by:** Maritime & Coastguard Agency (UK)
- **Enforcement:** Applied contractually during employment on ships; approved by regulatory agency.
- **Scope:** UK/International

### ILO — Maritime Labour Convention 2006 (MLC 2006)
- **Organization:** International Labour Organization (ILO)
- **Document:** Maritime Labour Convention, 2006 (Convention C186)
- **URL:** https://www.ilo.org/international-labour-standards/maritime-labour-convention-2006
- **Year:** Adopted 2006; entered into force August 20, 2013; amended 2016 (harassment/bullying provisions)
- **Key provisions relevant to conduct:** Right to freedom of association; elimination of forced labour; fair treatment standards; seafarer rights to promotion contingent on code of conduct compliance; prohibition of harassment and bullying (2016 amendment); fair treatment guidelines for seafarers detained in connection with alleged crimes.
- **Notes:** Not strictly a professional ethics code for individual seafarers but establishes the binding international framework for seafarer rights and conduct standards. Fourth pillar of international maritime law.
- **Enforcement:** Binding on flag states party to the convention; inspections by port state control.
- **Scope:** International

### IMPA — Professional Standards (Maritime Pilots)
- **Organization:** International Maritime Pilots' Association (IMPA)
- **Document:** IMPA Resolutions and Policies (professional standards)
- **URL:** https://www.impahq.org/impa-policies-publications
- **Year:** Ongoing; Resolutions updated July 2023
- **Key principles:** Promoting professional standards of pilotage worldwide; public interest served by regulated, cohesion pilotage free of commercial pressure; guidance on Master-Pilot Exchange; Diversity and Inclusion guidance.
- **Notes:** IMPA does not publish a dedicated standalone code of ethics. Professional standards are expressed through Resolutions and guidance documents. The International Standard for maritime Pilot Organizations (ISPO) through IALA provides overarching standards.
- **Enforcement:** Member association-level.
- **Scope:** International

### IMO — Code of Ethics for Personnel
- **Organization:** International Maritime Organization (IMO)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics for IMO Personnel
- **URL:** https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/OurWork/Documents/Code%20of%20Ethics%20for%20IMO%20Personnel.pdf
- **Year:** Current (references ICSC Standards applicable since 2012)
- **Key principles:** Loyalty to IMO; independence, impartiality, and integrity; prohibition on soliciting or accepting gifts; zero tolerance for fraud, corruption, and misconduct; prohibition of harassment including sexual harassment; highest standards of efficiency, competence, and integrity.
- **Notes:** This is an internal staff code, not a seafarer code. However, it represents the ethics framework of the intergovernmental body that sets international maritime standards, and is catalogued here as a key institutional document for Section H.
- **Scope:** International (IMO staff)

## Division 51 — Air Transport

### ALPA — Code of Ethics
- **Organization:** Air Line Pilots Association (ALPA)
- **Document:** ALPA Code of Ethics
- **URL:** https://www.alpa.org/about/code-of-ethics
- **Year:** Foundational code; updated version announced 2024/2025
- **Key principles:** Safety, comfort and well-being of passengers as first and greatest responsibility; faithful discharge of duties to employing airline; accepting responsibility of command; conduct on and off duty to instill confidence and respect; no false or malicious injury to professional reputation of other pilots; reporting professional incompetence to proper ALPA authorities.
- **Canons:** Multiple detailed canons providing specific guidance on professional conduct, safety, and pilot-to-pilot obligations.
- **Enforcement:** Required for ALPA membership; Professional Standards Committee (nearly 500 trained members) processes cases.
- **Scope:** US (North America; ALPA represents 79,000+ pilots)

### NATCA — Professional Air Traffic Controller's Code
- **Organization:** National Air Traffic Controllers Association (NATCA)
- **Document:** Professional Air Traffic Controller's Code; NATCA Code of Conduct
- **URL:** Code of Conduct: https://www.natca.org/code-of-conduct/; Controller's Code poster: https://www.natca.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/ATC-Code-Poster.pdf
- **Year:** Code poster 2020; Code of Conduct current
- **Key principles (Controller's Code):** Personal commitment to safety, excellence, and oath to public trust; conduct demonstrating trust; inspiring and motivating professionalism in others; safety of airspace system as greatest priority; individual actions represent the conduct of the entire profession.
- **Key principles (Code of Conduct):** Free environment from discrimination and harassment; zero-tolerance for harassing or discriminatory behavior.
- **Enforcement:** Professional Standards Committee (~500 trained members); thousands of cases processed.
- **Scope:** US

### IFALPA — Code of Conduct / Ethics
- **Organization:** International Federation of Air Line Pilots' Associations (IFALPA)
- **Document:** IFALPA Code of Conduct
- **URL:** https://www.ifalpa.org/
- **Year:** Current
- **Key principles:** Free environment from discrimination and harassment for all members; respectful conduct at all IFALPA activities; definitions of discrimination and harassment aligned with international standards.
- **Notes:** IFALPA has no standalone professional code of ethics for pilot conduct in the air; its explicit written code focuses on organizational conduct/harassment standards. Professional conduct is addressed through member association codes (e.g., ALPA). However, IFALPA's safety policies implicitly address ethics.
- **Scope:** International (70+ countries, 160,000+ pilots)

### AFA-CWA — Ethical Practices Code (Flight Attendants)
- **Organization:** Association of Flight Attendants – Communications Workers of America (AFA-CWA)
- **Document:** Ethical Practices Code
- **URL:** https://afaalaska.org/ethics (Alaska chapter; national code)
- **Year:** Current
- **Key principles:** Officers/representatives shall not receive compensation for serving on corporate boards as AFA-CWA representatives; prohibition on personal payments from employers or businesses doing business with AFA-CWA; prohibition on entering negotiations with management without Master Executive Council President's knowledge; prohibition on converting AFA-CWA property to personal advantage.
- **Notes:** Primarily a union officer ethics code rather than a professional conduct code for cabin crew behavior in the air. Operational professional standards are handled through EAP/Professional Standards Program.
- **Enforcement:** Union disciplinary process.
- **Scope:** US

### IATA — Code of Business Conduct
- **Organization:** International Air Transport Association (IATA)
- **Document:** Code of Business Conduct
- **URL:** https://www.iata.org/contentassets/c135b70ae5f34013b120b5008ca13369/code-business-conduct.pdf
- **Year:** Current
- **Key principles:** Highest standards of ethical conduct and social responsibilities; professional and ethical manner in all business operations; applies to all IATA employees, subsidiaries, and as far as possible to consultants, contractors, suppliers, and business partners.
- **Notes:** This is an organizational/staff code for IATA as an institution, not a code governing airline operations or pilot/crew conduct. However, it represents the leading international aviation industry trade body's ethics framework.
- **Scope:** International

### Aviators Model Code of Conduct (AMCC)
- **Organization:** Aviators Code Initiative (ACI) — independent volunteer initiative
- **Document:** Aviators Model Code of Conduct, Version 3
- **URL:** https://www.secureav.com/AMCC-v2.html; repository: https://repository.fit.edu/amccenglish/
- **Year:** Version 3 released 2022
- **Key principles:** Enhancing quality and safety in general aviation; promoting flight and ground safety, professionalism; encouraging good judgment, ethical behavior, and personal responsibility; supporting communication between pilots, regulators, and aviation community.
- **Notes:** Voluntary model code developed by aviation professionals, peer reviewed, endorsed by FAA resources. Not produced by a professional association or union but widely referenced.
- **Scope:** International (primarily general aviation)

## Division 52 — Warehousing and Support Activities for Transportation

### ISM — Principles and Standards of Ethical Supply Management Conduct
- **Organization:** Institute for Supply Management (ISM)
- **Document:** Principles and Standards of Ethical Supply Management Conduct with Guidelines
- **URL:** https://www.ismworld.org/globalassets/pub/docs/210_ethics_book.pdf; web version: https://www.ismworld.org/82780827-principles-and-standards-of-ethical-supply-mgmt/
- **Year:** Regularly reviewed/updated by the ISM Ethical Standards Committee (current)
- **Key principles:** Core ethical principles of integrity, value, and loyalty. Key standards: prevent unethical or compromising conduct; ensure personal/business activities do not conflict with employer's lawful interests; avoid behaviors that may influence supply management decisions; protect confidential/proprietary information; avoid improper reciprocal agreements; obey laws and trade agreements; develop professional skills.
- **Enforcement:** Member association requirement; applied through ISM certification programs.
- **Scope:** US (international influence)

### CIPS — Code of Conduct (Procurement and Supply)
- **Organization:** Chartered Institute of Procurement and Supply (CIPS)
- **Document:** CIPS Code of Conduct for Members
- **URL:** https://www.cips.org/about-us/cips-code-of-conduct
- **Year:** Current (2024 version; significant amendments from 2014 onward)
- **Key principles:** Five commitments — (1) Enhance and protect the standing of the profession; (2) Maintain highest integrity in business relationships; (3) Promote eradication of unethical business practices (including modern slavery, fraud, corruption); (4) Enhance professional proficiency; (5) Ensure legal compliance.
- **Enforcement:** Formal Disciplinary Committee; sanctions range from written reprimand to membership expulsion. Online ethics test (since 2014). Professional Register for public verification.
- **Royal Charter authority:** Breaches judged against code; action taken under disciplinary rules of CIPS Royal Charter.
- **Scope:** International (UK-based; global chartered body)

### SCMA — Code of Ethics for Supply Chain Management Professionals
- **Organization:** Supply Chain Management Association / Supply Chain Canada (SCMA)
- **Document:** SCMA Code of Ethics for Professionals in the Field of Supply Chain Management
- **URL:** https://www.supplychaincanada.com/media/files/code-of-ethics.pdf; web: https://scma.com/on/about/code-of-ethics
- **Year:** Current (reviewed minimum every 5 years)
- **Key principles:** Conduct becoming of a professional; exercise professional judgment to avoid conflicts of interest with mandatory disclosure; no misrepresentation of credentials; no irregular admission to membership; compliance with highest supply management standards.
- **Enforcement:** Sanctions include reprimand, letter of censure (to member, Institute, and SCMA National Office); censured members barred from holding office for one year.
- **Scope:** Canada (international recognition)

### NCBFAA — Code of Ethics (Customs Brokers and Freight Forwarders)
- **Organization:** National Customs Brokers & Forwarders Association of America (NCBFAA)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics (referenced in bylaws and membership requirements)
- **URL:** https://www.ncbfaa.org/about-ncbfaa (bylaws PDF linked); https://members.ncbfaa.org/4DCGI/cms/review.html?Action=CMS_Document&DocID=8574
- **Year:** Current
- **Key principles:** Highest standards of professional excellence in international trade; professionalism based on integrity, principles, ethics, and trust; adherence required for Certified Customs Specialists (CCS).
- **Notes:** The NCBFAA code is referenced within bylaws rather than as a standalone published document. Its core content is honesty, accuracy, and compliance in customs and freight operations.
- **Enforcement:** Membership requirement; CCS certification holders must adhere.
- **Scope:** US

## Division 53 — Postal and Courier Activities

*Note: Postal worker unions (NALC, APWU) focus on labor rights and collective bargaining; no standalone professional ethics code found. Courier/logistics professionals at the supply chain level are covered by ISM and CIPS above (Div 52 overlap).*

**Gap noted:** No specific professional ethics code found for postal delivery workers or courier services. USPS employees are subject to federal ethics regulations (Standards of Ethical Conduct for Employees of the Executive Branch, 5 CFR Part 2635), but this is a government regulation, not a professional association code. APWU and NALC constitutions contain member obligations but no dedicated ethics code.

## Coverage Assessment

**Well-covered professions:**
- Airline pilots: ALPA (strong), IFALPA, AMCC
- Air traffic controllers: NATCA (strong)
- Flight attendants: AFA-CWA
- Aviation industry body: IATA
- Seafarers/Merchant Navy: Nautilus/RMT Code of Conduct, ILO MLC 2006
- Maritime pilots: IMPA professional standards
- Maritime organization: IMO personnel code
- Supply chain/procurement: ISM (strong), CIPS (strong, UK), SCMA (Canada), NCBFAA

**Gaps / Not found:**
- Land transport: No professional ethics code for truck drivers (OOIDA has no ethics code; ATA code limited to Moving & Storage)
- Rail: SMART-TD and UTU/railroad unions have constitutions with member obligations but no dedicated ethics code
- Bus/transit drivers: No professional code; APTA governance code only
- Pipeline transport: No dedicated professional ethics code found
- Postal/courier workers: No dedicated professional ethics code found (government employees covered by federal ethics regulations)
- Airport ground handling: No dedicated code found
