**Notes on 68.1:**
- NAR Code of Ethics (adopted 1913, continuously updated) is one of the oldest and most comprehensive professional codes in real estate. 2026 edition includes anti-harassment provisions tied to protected class status. Enforcement through local/state REALTOR® boards with arbitration.
- CREA REALTOR® Code (first adopted 1959) mirrors NAR structure; requires written disclosure of agency relationships and conflicts of interest.
- REIA administers National Principles of Conduct through state/territory affiliates; acts as Australian FIABCI chapter.
- FIABCI Code of Ethics is the global framework; member country associations (including REIA, NAR, CREA) align with it. FIABCI is also a signatory to the International Ethics Standards Coalition.
- RICS Rules of Conduct replaced the earlier Rules of Conduct for Members and Global Ethical Principles effective February 2022. Covers honesty, integrity, competence, service, respect, and responsibility. Mandatory CPD for chartered status.

**Notes on 68.2:**
- IREM Code of Professional Ethics (founded 1933) covers fiduciary duty, loyalty to client, non-commingling of funds, prohibition on material misrepresentation. IREM also maintains a separate AMO (Accredited Management Organization) Code for firms — both are separately enforceable.
- NARPM Code (adopted 1994, amended several times) covers duty to public, non-discrimination, fiduciary responsibility, confidentiality, and fair housing compliance. The FTC (2014) required NARPM to remove provisions that limited competition.

**Notes on 68.3:**
- Appraisal Institute Code of Professional Ethics is organized around Canons (including independence of analysis, public protection, and non-detrimental conduct toward the profession). Updated May 2022; 2025 version also available.
- ASA Principles of Appraisal Practice and Code of Ethics (2020 edition) covers honesty, impartiality, competency, confidentiality, conflict avoidance, and five-year record retention. All ASA members must subscribe.
- USPAP Ethics Rule (2024 edition expanded) is quasi-regulatory (required by law for licensed appraisers in all US states). The Conduct section prohibits predetermined value opinions; 2024 update expanded non-discrimination obligations. This is the most legally enforceable ethics framework in real estate.

**Notes on 68.4:**
- NAMB Code of Ethics requires honesty, integrity, professional conduct, transparent communication, and honest advertising. The NAMB L.I.S.A. (Lending Integrity Seal of Approval) adds continuing education, criminal background check, and ethics grievance process.
- MBA national organization has a Code of Conduct; state affiliates (Alabama, Kansas, Tennessee, New Jersey, etc.) maintain Canons of Ethics covering non-discrimination, candor and fairness, timely loan processing, integrity of loan submissions, and confidentiality.
- NRMLA (National Reverse Mortgage Lenders Association) also maintains a Code of Ethics & Professional Responsibility covering the reverse mortgage subspecialty.

- NAIOP: no public-facing ethics code located; NAIOP is primarily a developer/owner association rather than an individual professional designation body.

## Research Notes and Gaps

- **Real estate agents/brokers:** Richest ethics infrastructure of any Section L subsector. NAR is the anchor (US, 1.5M+ members); CREA (Canada), REIA (Australia), and RICS (UK/global) provide geographic coverage. FIABCI provides international coordination.
- **Property managers:** Two-tier enforcement — individual (IREM CPM Code) and corporate (AMO Code). NARPM covers the residential specialty.
- **Appraisers:** Three-layer framework — USPAP (quasi-regulatory, legally required), Appraisal Institute (designation holders), ASA (multi-asset appraisers). USPAP is the most legally enforceable code in real estate.
- **Mortgage lending:** NAMB (brokers) and MBA affiliates (lenders) cover different segments. NRMLA covers reverse mortgages (specialty).
- **Commercial real estate:** CCIM and SIOR both have designation-linked ethics codes aligned with NAR framework. NAIOP is a gap.
- **International gap:** ICREA (International Consortium of Real Estate Associations) was not found to maintain its own code separate from member associations (NAR, CREA, REIA).
- **Cross-listing:** RICS is also found in Section F (construction/surveying) but has significant application in Section L (real estate valuation and management).
