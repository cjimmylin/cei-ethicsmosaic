## Cross-Sector / Notes on Gaps

- **Banking trade bodies:** The European Banking Federation (EBF), British Bankers' Association (now merged into UK Finance), and American Bankers Association advocacy arm are trade associations without standalone member codes of ethics — their ethics framing is embedded in regulatory compliance (FCA PRIN for UK; Basel for international).
- **Reinsurance:** No standalone reinsurance-specific professional ethics code found. Reinsurance underwriters fall under general CPCU / IFoA / actuarial codes.
- **FinTech / cryptocurrency:** Not yet covered by dedicated professional ethics codes from established bodies. Some codes (FINRA, FCA, MAS) are being extended to cover crypto intermediaries by regulation.
- **Islamic finance:** FSPB Code (entry 4) explicitly covers Islamic finance practitioners in Malaysia and Singapore.
- **Pension trustee codes:** CFA Institute Pension Trustee Code (entry 30) is the most significant international model, though uptake varies by jurisdiction.

### Key Observations

1. **Finance is the most heavily coded sector found so far** — actuaries alone have 7 distinct codes (5 US bodies + UK IFoA + Australia Actuaries Institute + IAA international) all closely coordinated.
2. **Statutory codes are rare but exist** — FASEA Australia (2019) and FCA Principles for Businesses (UK) are statutory/quasi-statutory, making them legally enforceable rather than self-regulatory.
3. **IESBA serves as the global backbone** for accountants and financial professionals (adopted by 180+ IFAC member bodies) and should be treated as the international_model anchor for financial sector ethics.
4. **CFA Institute is uniquely prolific** — it maintains the Code of Ethics + Standards of Professional Conduct, GIPS standards, Asset Manager Code, and Pension Trustee Code, making it the most influential ethics-producing body for investment professionals globally.
5. **Risk management has two competing bodies** — RIMS (US-focused, certification-based) and IRM (UK/global, membership-based) with GARP and PRMIA serving the quantitative/FRM community.
6. **Insurance adjusters are well-organized** — NAIIA (independent) and NAPIA (public) both maintain distinct and detailed codes covering their separate roles in the claims process.
