# Section S — Other Service Activities
## Research Notes (ISIC Rev. 4, Divisions 94–96)

**Date:** 2026-03-21
**Researcher:** Claude Code (agent)
**Wave:** 4

---

## Overview

Section S covers membership organizations (Div. 94), computer/goods repair (Div. 95), and personal services (Div. 96). Division 94 is particularly interesting because membership organizations are themselves major *issuers* of ethics codes — ASAE (association management professionals) governs this sector. Division 96 (personal services) yields a surprising number of ethics codes from massage therapy, esthetics, cosmetology, matchmaking, and funeral services.

---

## Division 94 — Activities of Membership Organizations

### ASAE — American Society of Association Executives
- **Document:** Standards of Conduct / Code of Conduct
- **URL:** https://www.asaecenter.org/about-us/policies/code-of-conduct
- **PDF (current standards):** https://www.asaecenter.org/-/media/ASAE/Files/AboutASAE/Standards-Of-Conduct-SEPT-23-2022.ashx
- **Ethics Toolkit:** https://www.asaecenter.org/-/media/models-and-samples/6/6/1/5/ASAE-Ethics-Toolkit-March-2025.ashx
- **Notes:** Governs professional association executives and staff. Core Ethical Standards include: basic conduct, confidentiality, competition, giving and receiving, and other integrity provisions. ASAE is the professional association of association executives — meta-level governance for the entire membership organization sector.
- **Scope:** US (international chapters)
- **Enforcement:** Self-regulatory; membership condition; disciplinary process

### AFL-CIO — American Federation of Labor and Congress of Industrial Organizations
- **Document:** Code of Conduct
- **URL:** https://aflcio.org/media/2073 (main page)
- **PDF:** https://aflcio.org/sites/default/files/2018-02/AFL-CIO%20Code%20of%20Conduct%20-%20Final.pdf
- **Notes:** Applicable to all attendees at AFL-CIO events, activities, meetings, and workplaces. Covers anti-discrimination, anti-harassment, and professional conduct. AFL-CIO Constitution (Article X) also requires affiliated unions to adopt their own ethical practices codes. Federation-level code for 12.5 million members across affiliated unions.
- **Scope:** US
- **Enforcement:** Institutional enforcement; affiliates required to adopt own codes

### TUC — Trades Union Congress (UK) [Not found as standalone code]
- **Notes:** TUC does not publish a standalone code of ethics in the same tradition as US professional associations. General conduct is governed through individual union rulebooks and the TUC Constitution. No dedicated public TUC ethics code found.
- **Status:** Gap — TUC is a federation that delegates ethics governance to affiliated unions

## Division 94 — Religious Organizations (Note)

Religious organization governance (churches, faith-based nonprofits) rarely produces a single standalone ethics code applicable to all staff/volunteers — instead, individual denominations publish doctrinal standards. Specific examples exist (e.g., National Council of Churches, faith-based nonprofit governance frameworks) but these overlap heavily with Section O (Public Administration/social services) and represent denomination-specific rather than sector-wide standards. No single representative "religious organization staff code of ethics" was identified for inclusion.

## Division 95 — Repair of Computers and Personal/Household Goods

### CompTIA — Computing Technology Industry Association
- **Document:** Candidate Code of Ethics (for CompTIA-certified IT professionals)
- **URL:** https://www.comptia.org/en-us/resources/test-policies/continuing-education-policies/candidate-code-of-ethics/
- **Notes:** All CompTIA-certified persons must agree to the code. Covers: no dishonesty/fraud/deceit with clients or employers; no unauthorized IT system breach; no cheating on exams; no behavior compromising exam integrity. Particularly relevant for IT repair technicians (Div. 95) as A+, Network+ certifications are common in repair/tech support roles. Note: CompTIA was also found in J62 (ICT) for cybersecurity — cross-list.
- **Scope:** International (US-based)
- **Enforcement:** Certification revocation possible; CompTIA Certification Candidate Agreement
- **Cross-listed with:** J — ICT (cybersecurity/IT professionals)

### iFixit
- **Document:** No formal code of ethics found
- **Notes:** iFixit advocates for the right to repair and publishes repair guides, but does not maintain a formal professional code of ethics. It is an advocacy organization, not a professional association with membership-based ethical standards.
- **Status:** No code found

## Division 96 — Other Personal Service Activities

### AMTA — American Massage Therapy Association
- **Document:** Code of Ethics (with Standards of Practice)
- **URL:** https://www.amtamassage.org/about/core-documents/
- **PDF direct:** https://tn.wp.amtamassage.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/50/2011/03/code_of_ethics_AMTA.pdf
- **Notes:** Membership prerequisite is adherence to the Code. Two tiers: Principles of Ethics (aspirational) and Rules of Ethics (mandatory). Covers professional conduct, client welfare, scope of practice, confidentiality.
- **Scope:** US
- **Enforcement:** Membership revocation possible; disciplinary process exists

### ABMP — Associated Bodywork and Massage Professionals
- **Document:** Code of Ethics for Massage Therapists
- **URL:** https://www.abmp.com/abmp-code-ethics
- **Notes:** Parallel to AMTA — ABMP is the second major US massage therapy membership organization. Code covers client dignity, confidentiality, professional boundaries, scope of practice. Membership binding.
- **Scope:** US
- **Enforcement:** Membership requirement

### NCEA — National Coalition of Estheticians Associations
- **Document:** Code of Ethics
- **URL:** https://nceacertified.org/ncea-national-esthetician-association-code-of-ethics/
- **Notes:** Covers licensed estheticians. Core areas: client confidentiality, scope of practice, professional conduct, honest representation of qualifications, legal compliance. NCEA Certified (NC) credential linked to the code.
- **Scope:** US
- **Enforcement:** Certification requirement (NCEA Certified credential)

### Professional Beauty Association (PBA, formerly NCA)
- **Document:** Code of Ethical Practices (sector-specific versions)
- **URL:** https://www.probeauty.org/ (pledge at probeauty.org/pledge)
- **Notes:** PBA absorbed the National Cosmetology Association (NCA). Separate codes for: manufacturers, distributors, salon/spa owners, and licensed professionals. Code covers health/safety standards, professionalism, ethical behavior. NCA's heritage is incorporated.
- **Scope:** US
- **Enforcement:** Self-regulatory; membership condition

### NFDA — National Funeral Directors Association [CROSS-LISTING from G47]
- **Document:** Code of Professional Conduct
- **URL:** https://nfda.org/about-nfda/code-of-professional-conduct
- **PDF:** https://nfda.org/Portals/0/NFDAORG/About/Professional_Conduct/Code-of-Professional-Conduct.pdf
- **Notes:** Funeral services are classifiable in G47 (retail mortuary goods) or Div. 96 (personal services/funeral services). Code covers: obligations to the family, care of the decedent, public obligations, government compliance, NFDA member obligations. Originally captured in prior wave G47 analysis — cross-list here.
- **Scope:** US
- **Cross-listed from:** G — Wholesale and Retail Trade

### Matchmaking Institute (MAIA — formerly)
- **Document:** Matchmakers Network Code of Ethics
- **URL:** https://matchmakinginstitute.com/code_of_ethics/
- **Notes:** Sets values, principles, and standards for Certified Matchmakers. Core values: customer service, dignity of the person, importance of human relationships, integrity, competence. Governs relationships with clients, colleagues, and the profession. Addresses self-determination and client welfare.
- **Scope:** US / International
- **Enforcement:** Certification requirement (Certified Matchmaker credential)

### Alliance of Professional Tattooists (APT)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics (referenced in membership descriptions)
- **URL:** https://safe-tattoos.com/ (main site; specific ethics page not located in search)
- **Notes:** APT maintains ethical standards for tattoo artists focused on bloodborne pathogen safety, sterilization, professional conduct. A dedicated public ethics page was not found in search results. The organization focuses heavily on safety education (https://www.tattoosafety.org/) with ethics embedded in safety standards.
- **Status:** Confirmed ethical standards exist; no standalone public ethics URL found

## Search Gaps / Not Found

- **TUC (UK trade union federation):** No standalone ethics code; delegates to affiliated unions.
- **Political parties (ISIC 94.92):** By nature, political parties do not publish professional ethics codes applicable to members/staff in the same sense as professional associations.
- **iFixit:** Advocacy organization only; no professional code.
- **Alliance of Professional Tattooists:** Safety-focused; ethics embedded but no standalone public URL.
- **International beauty guild equivalents:** The Guild of Professional Beauty Therapists (UK) has a code at beautyguild.com/Membership/Code-Of-Ethics but this is UK-specific and not confirmed in depth.

## Assessment

Division 94 yields ASAE (strong, authoritative) and AFL-CIO (broad union-sector coverage). Division 96 is the richest: massage therapy alone has two major competing associations (AMTA and ABMP), esthetics has NCEA, cosmetology/hair has PBA/NCA, funeral services has NFDA (cross-list), and matchmaking has the Matchmaking Institute. This section is more coverage-rich than expected. Division 95 (repair) has only CompTIA (which was already in J62).

**Total confirmed entries for Section S:** 10 organizations (including cross-listings)
