# Section G — Wholesale and Retail Trade

**ISIC Rev 4 Divisions 45–47**
**Researcher notes:** Trade sectors — motor vehicles, wholesale, and retail — have a patchwork ethics infrastructure. Motor vehicles stands out with two strong national codes (NADA, NIADA) and an auto auction code (NAAA). Wholesale trade is thin: NAW has no public code, NAWLA has no public code, and HDA lacks a public ethics document. The strongest wholesale ethics codes exist in specialty sub-sectors: direct selling (DSA) is comprehensive and externally enforced; pawnbrokers (NPA) updated their code in 2024. Retail trade has rich specialty professional codes: funeral directors (NFDA), art dealers (ADAA), jewelers (AGTA, JVC/RJC), and appraisers (AAA) all have substantive codes. The NRF and RILA are primarily lobbying bodies without member-facing professional ethics codes. Meeting planners (MPI, PCMA) straddle Section G commerce and Section I hospitality but are classified here under retail services.

## Division 45 — Wholesale and Retail Trade and Repair of Motor Vehicles

### NADA — Code of Ethics (National Automobile Dealers Association)
- **Organization:** National Automobile Dealers Association (NADA)
- **Document:** NADA/ATD Code of Ethics + Ethics Guide
- **URL:** https://www.nada.org/nada/code-ethics
- **Ethics Guide:** https://www.nada.org/nada/about-nada/code-of-ethics/ethics-guide
- **Year:** Current (actively maintained; Guide updated 2020s)
- **Key principles:** Treat customers fairly, openly and honestly; comply with anti-discrimination laws; represent products clearly and factually; stand fully behind warranties; professionalism in meeting transportation needs. Ethics Guide covers four operational areas: sales, service, financial services, and advertising.
- **Enforcement:** Membership requirement; NADA/ATD member commitment. WANADA (state affiliate) issued updated version signal ongoing revision.
- **Scope:** US (national franchise dealers)
- **Org type:** Industry trade association

### NIADA — Code of Ethics (National Independent Automobile Dealers Association)
- **Organization:** National Independent Automobile Dealers Association (NIADA)
- **Document:** NIADA Code of Ethics
- **URL:** https://niada.com/about/ (embedded in About page)
- **Year:** Active (originated 1947 as NUCDA; continuously maintained)
- **Key principles:** Duty of integrity, honor and fair dealing toward the general public; compliance with all city, county, state and federal laws; employ truth and accuracy in advertising and selling; expose and halt deceptive schemes; stand behind guarantees given with vehicle sales; constantly improve business methods.
- **Enforcement:** Membership condition; all members agree to be bound by the code.
- **Scope:** US (independent/used vehicle dealers, 38,000+ members)
- **Org type:** Industry trade association

### NAAA — Code of Ethics (National Auto Auction Association)
- **Organization:** National Auto Auction Association (NAAA)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics (Restated 2022)
- **URL:** https://www.naaa.com/membership/membership_pdfs/Code_of_Ethics_Restated_2-15-22.pdf
- **Mission page:** https://www.naaa.com/pages/mission.html
- **Year:** Restated February 15, 2022
- **Key principles:** Maintaining highest ethical standards in the wholesale auto auction industry; leadership in standards and ethics for evolving remarketing practices. Hall of Fame inductees must have consistently followed the code's high standards.
- **Enforcement:** Membership requirement; referenced in industry awards eligibility.
- **Scope:** US (wholesale auto auction industry)
- **Org type:** Industry trade association

## Division 46 — Wholesale Trade

### NAW — No Public Code Found
- **Organization:** National Association of Wholesaler-Distributors (NAW)
- **Finding:** NAW is primarily a lobbying and research body. No public code of ethics or code of conduct was located. The organization does publish ethics-adjacent AI guidance but has no formal member-facing ethical code.
- **Status:** Gap — no qualifying document found.

### NAWLA — No Public Code Found
- **Organization:** North American Wholesale Lumber Association (NAWLA, founded 1893)
- **Finding:** NAWLA's historical records reference early industry ethics discussions (1893s founding era) but no current public code of ethics was located. The organization's website does not host a formal ethics code.
- **Status:** Gap — no qualifying document found.

### HDA — No Public Code Found
- **Organization:** Healthcare Distribution Alliance (HDA)
- **Finding:** HDA represents primary pharmaceutical distributors. No public code of ethics document was located on their website. Organization advocates for supply chain standards through regulatory channels rather than publishing a member ethics code.
- **Status:** Gap — no qualifying document found.

## Division 47 — Retail Trade

### DSA — Code of Ethics (Direct Selling Association)
- **Organization:** Direct Selling Association (DSA)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics
- **URL:** https://www.dsa.org/consumerprotection/code-of-ethics
- **PDF:** https://www.dsa.org/docs/default-source/advocacy/dsa_codeofethics.pdf
- **Year:** Enacted 1970; continuously updated (2018 compliance edition available)
- **Key principles:** No deceptive, false, unethical or unlawful consumer or recruiting practices; accurate product and earnings representations; consumer privacy protection; cooling-off and buyback rights; truthful testimonials; no pyramid scheme structures; independent salesperson conduct standards.
- **Enforcement:** Independent Code Administrator enforces complaints; compliance is initial and continuing membership condition; binding on all member companies.
- **Scope:** US (direct selling companies and their salespeople)
- **Org type:** Industry trade association (self-regulatory body with independent administrator)

### NPA — Code of Ethical Integrity (National Pawnbrokers Association)
- **Organization:** National Pawnbrokers Association (NPA)
- **Document:** Code of Ethical Integrity (updated 2024; previous version 2007)
- **URL:** https://www.nationalpawnbrokers.org/code-of-ethics/
- **Year:** 2024 (most recent update)
- **Key principles:** Transact business honestly and forthrightly; maintain business premises with distinction and professionalism; obey all local, state, and federal laws and assist in prosecution of violators; engage in fair and vigorous competition; serve the community and pawn industry with integrity.
- **Enforcement:** Membership is dependent on adherence to the code; reviewed and updated 2024 for "next generation."
- **Scope:** US (independent pawnbrokers, founded 1987)
- **Org type:** Industry trade association

### NFDA — Code of Professional Conduct (National Funeral Directors Association)
- **Organization:** National Funeral Directors Association (NFDA)
- **Document:** Code of Professional Conduct
- **URL:** https://nfda.org/about-nfda/code-of-professional-conduct
- **PDF:** https://nfda.org/Portals/0/NFDAORG/About/Professional_Conduct/Code-of-Professional-Conduct.pdf
- **Year:** Approved October 19, 2002 (actively enforced; referenced as current)
- **Key principles:** Five areas of obligation — to the Family (full consumer disclosure, honest pricing, dignity of remains), to the Care of the Decedent (professional handling, no misrepresentation), to the Public (accurate advertising, no defamation), to the Government (legal compliance, truthful death certificates), and to NFDA (supporting the association's mission). Explicitly prohibits false statements on death certificates and misleading advertising.
- **Enforcement:** Binding on all NFDA Firm, Individual, Retired and Student Members; violations subject to disciplinary action under NFDA Constitution and Bylaws.
- **Scope:** US (funeral directors and funeral homes)
- **Org type:** Professional/trade association

### ADAA — Code of Ethics and Professional Practices (Art Dealers Association of America)
- **Organization:** Art Dealers Association of America (ADAA, founded 1962)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics and Professional Practices
- **URL:** https://artdealers.org/about/code-of-ethics-and-professional-practices
- **PDF:** https://www.obs-traffic.museum/sites/default/files/ressources/files/ADAA_Code_Ethics.pdf
- **Year:** Current (continuously maintained since founding)
- **Key principles:** Honest dealings with artists, clients, colleagues and the public; courtesy and respect in all transactions; no defamation of other dealers; gallery management compliance with employment laws (non-discrimination, anti-harassment); accurate provenance and condition representations; good faith response to concerns raised by other members; applies to all transactions including art fair participation.
- **Enforcement:** Member expectation; receptive cooperation in complaint resolution; applies to all art fair and gallery transactions.
- **Scope:** US (commercial art dealers)
- **Org type:** Professional/trade association

### AGTA — Code of Ethics and Principles of Fair Business Practice (American Gem Trade Association)
- **Organization:** American Gem Trade Association (AGTA, founded 1981)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics and Principles of Fair Business Practice
- **URL:** https://agta.org/about/
- **PDF:** https://agta.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/AGTA-COE-2016.pdf
- **Year:** 1984 (established); 2016 edition (most recent PDF)
- **Key principles:** Disclosure of gemstone enhancements on all commercial documents; fair business practices in buying, selling, and trading natural colored gemstones, pearls, natural colored diamonds, and colored stone manufactured items; compliance with AGTA Gemstone Information Manual; compliance with all local, state and federal laws. Stricter disclosure requirements than FTC minimums.
- **Enforcement:** Annual commitment by all AGTA Firm and Affiliate Members; Ethics & Grievance Committee.
- **Scope:** US (colored gemstone and pearl trade)
- **Org type:** Industry trade association

### RJC — Code of Practices (Responsible Jewellery Council)
- **Organization:** Responsible Jewellery Council (RJC)
- **Document:** Code of Practices (COP) 2024
- **URL:** https://www.responsiblejewellery.com/standards/code-of-practices/
- **PDF (2019):** https://www.responsiblejewellery.com/wp-content/uploads/RJC-COP-December-2019.pdf
- **Year:** 2024 (fully revised; effective January 1, 2025; prior versions 2019, 2013)
- **Key principles:** Multi-attribute standard covering business ethics, human rights, labor rights, health and safety, environmental performance, and management systems across the full jewellery supply chain from mine to retail. Mandatory for all commercial members within two years of joining.
- **Enforcement:** Third-party certification required (audited by accredited certification bodies); mandatory for all RJC commercial members; next revision due 2029.
- **Scope:** International (full supply chain — mining, trading, manufacturing, retail)
- **Org type:** Multi-stakeholder standards body / certification body

### AAA — Code of Ethics (Appraisers Association of America)
- **Organization:** Appraisers Association of America (AAA, established 1949)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics
- **URL:** https://www.appraisersassociation.org/about-us (embedded; see About page)
- **Year:** Current
- **Key principles:** Independent valuation outside third-party influences; no outside interest in subject property; appraisal work only within areas of professional expertise; objective value conclusions considering all factors; highest standards of connoisseurship in examining and documenting property; all appraisals compliant with USPAP (Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice).
- **Enforcement:** Required for all 900+ members across 100 specialization areas; USPAP compliance mandatory.
- **Scope:** US (personal property appraisers specializing in fine and decorative arts, antiques)
- **Org type:** Professional association

### NAA — Code of Ethics (National Auctioneers Association)
- **Organization:** National Auctioneers Association (NAA)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics
- **URL:** https://www.auctioneers.org/code%20of%20ethics
- **Year:** Current (actively enforced)
- **Key principles:** Eight core principles — Integrity (personal and professional), Respect (diversity and mutual respect), Fiduciary responsibility (competent and ethical client services), Compliance (upholding laws and the Code), Confidentiality (protecting client information), Loyalty (to NAA mission), Duty (good faith with the public), and Professionalism (excellence in dealings with auction professionals). Felony conviction in auction work = automatic code violation.
- **Enforcement:** Written grievance process; CEO + Chairman of Board Grievance Committee; discipline ranges from censure to dismissal; also has separate CAI (Certified Auctioneers Institute) Code of Conduct.
- **Scope:** US (professional auctioneers)
- **Org type:** Professional association

### JVC — Ethics Standards (Jewelers Vigilance Committee)
- **Organization:** Jewelers Vigilance Committee (JVC, founded 1917)
- **Document:** Ethics pledge and standards (members pledge to comply with all applicable laws and make accurate representations)
- **URL:** https://jvclegal.org/
- **Year:** Current (founding mission; ongoing)
- **Key principles:** Highest ethical standards; compliance with all laws applicable to jewelry industry; accurate representations about products sold; works with FTC, U.S. Customs, Dept of State on industry compliance; educational tools for legal compliance; dispute resolution for consumers and trade.
- **Enforcement:** Member pledge; not-for-profit trade association watchdog role; works with government agencies.
- **Scope:** US (jewelry industry)
- **Org type:** Trade watchdog / not-for-profit association
- **Note:** JVC functions as an industry legal compliance and ethics watchdog rather than issuing a standalone formal code of ethics document. RJC (above) provides the stronger international certification standard for the jewelry supply chain.
