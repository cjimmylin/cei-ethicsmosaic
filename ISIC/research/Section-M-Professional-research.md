### Key Observations

1. **Division 69 (Legal/Accounting)** is the richest, driven by the legal profession's multi-jurisdictional coverage (US/UK/Australia/Canada/International) and accountancy's well-developed international standard-setting ecosystem (IESBA as apex body).

2. **Division 71 (Engineering/Architecture)** is equally rich because multiple distinct engineering disciplines each have their own associations, plus international umbrella bodies (WFEO, FIDIC, UIA).

3. **Division 72 (Research)** has fewer formal codes but produces uniquely authoritative international documents (Declaration of Helsinki, Singapore Statement, ALLEA Code) that carry de facto international weight across all research disciplines.

4. **Division 73 (Advertising/Market Research)** features a mix: advertising codes tend to be self-regulatory/aspirational, while market research codes (MRS, ESOMAR/ICC) are more binding with enforcement mechanisms.

5. **Division 75 (Veterinary)** has a clear three-tier structure: national statutory bodies (RCVS/UK), national professional associations (AVMA, AVA), and international model codes (WVA, FVE).

6. **Enforcement range:** Statutory (RCVS, SRA, BSB) → self_regulatory_binding (most professional associations) → aspirational/voluntary (international model codes, research integrity statements).

### Gaps / Areas for Further Research

- **Canada engineering:** Engineers Canada Code of Ethics
- **Canada architecture:** Royal Architectural Institute of Canada code
- **EU legal profession:** CCBE (Council of Bars and Law Societies of Europe) code
- **India, South Africa legal:** not searched
- **Film/video production** (ISIC 74): no dedicated professional ethics code found
- **Sports scientists, kinesiology:** possibly cross-listed with health professions
