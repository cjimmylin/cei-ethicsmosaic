# Section I — Accommodation and Food Service Activities

**ISIC Rev 4 Divisions 55–56**
**Researcher notes:** Hospitality has a more developed professional ethics infrastructure than might be expected for a service sector. The strongest codes come from food service professions with certification programs: ACF (chefs) has a formally published PDF code, IFSEA (food service executives, est. 1901) is the oldest professional association with ethics standards, and NACE (catering and events) has an enforceable member code. Convention and meeting planning has two codes of substance: PCMA's Principles of Professional and Ethical Conduct and MPI's Principles of Professionalism. The accommodation side is thinner: AHLA has no public member ethics code, but the merged ALP (formerly PAII innkeepers + AIHP) adopted a new formal Code of Ethics. Les Clefs d'Or has a detailed concierge code with conflict-of-interest and confidentiality provisions. The Court of Master Sommeliers has a signed Code of Ethics and Professional Responsibility required for all participants. The National Restaurant Association has only an event-level conduct policy, not a professional ethics code for restaurateurs.

## Division 55 — Accommodation

### Les Clefs d'Or USA — Code of Ethics and Professional Standards
- **Organization:** Les Clefs d'Or USA (Union Internationale des Concierges d'Hôtels — UICH, international; US section est. ~1980s)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics and Professional Standards
- **URL:** https://www.lcdusa.org/ethics-and-professional-standards/
- **Australian chapter (for comparison):** https://www.lesclefsdoraustralia.org/code-of-ethics
- **International:** https://www.lesclefsdor.org/
- **Year:** Current (annually renewed via signed oath)
- **Key principles:** Legal compliance at all times; professional integrity (no disparagement of colleagues); vendor relations (use reputable businesses, treat vendors with respect and professionalism); avoid conflicts of interest (no commissions from restaurants, no incentive-driven competitions); confidentiality (guest information, employer business information); annual oath signing required. Founded under motto "In Service Through Friendship" (est. Paris 1929).
- **Enforcement:** Annual oath required; member conduct monitored by chapter and national boards; formal ethics standards upheld as membership condition.
- **Scope:** International/US (hotel concierges; golden keys designation)
- **Org type:** Professional association

### ALP — Code of Ethics (Association of Lodging Professionals)
- **Organization:** Association of Lodging Professionals (ALP) — successor to PAII (Professional Association of Innkeepers International) + AIHP merger
- **Document:** Code of Ethics (newly adopted)
- **URL:** https://www.alplodging.org/alp-code-of-ethics/
- **Year:** Recent adoption (announced post-merger)
- **Key principles:** Aspirational code — best efforts to achieve high ethical standards; responsibilities and duties to guests, employees, vendors and suppliers, the Association, and the public; crafted in consultation with an attorney specializing in association law.
- **Enforcement:** All ALP Members required to consent to the Code of Ethics as membership condition.
- **Scope:** US (innkeepers, B&B owners, boutique hotel operators)
- **Org type:** Professional/trade association

### MPI — Principles of Professionalism (Meeting Professionals International)
- **Organization:** Meeting Professionals International (MPI)
- **Document:** Principles of Professionalism
- **URL:** https://www.mpi.org/about/leadership-governance/principles-of-professionalism
- **Year:** Current (board-approved governance document)
- **Key principles:** Ethics and Transparency (honesty, dependability, avoiding conflicts of interest and personal gain); Embracing Diversity (inclusive business climate, respect regardless of origin, race, religion, gender, age, disability, sexual orientation); Anti-harassment (zero tolerance for harassment based on any protected characteristic); Sustainability (responsible resource use in meeting production); no business relationships with organizations involved in human trafficking or commercial sexual exploitation of children.
- **Enforcement:** Membership principles; MPI Policy Manual (revised September 2023) provides governance framework.
- **Scope:** International (meeting and event planning professionals)
- **Org type:** Professional association

### PCMA — Principles of Professional and Ethical Conduct (Professional Convention Management Association)
- **Organization:** Professional Convention Management Association (PCMA)
- **Document:** Principles of Professional and Ethical Conduct
- **URL:** https://www.pcma.org/about/principles-of-professional-and-ethical-conduct/
- **PDF (Chesapeake chapter):** https://chesapeake.pcma.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2017/02/code_of_ethics.pdf
- **Year:** Current (continuously maintained)
- **Key principles:** Conduct with integrity, respect, honesty and credibility; highest ethical standards in all meetings; negotiate agreements in good faith; respect policies and regulations of all parties; pursue continuing education and share knowledge; avoid activities causing damage or discredit to one's organization or the industry; avoid conflicts of interest.
- **Enforcement:** Membership principles; referenced in continuing professional development.
- **Scope:** International/US (convention and meetings management professionals)
- **Org type:** Professional association

### GBTA — Code of Ethics (Global Business Travel Association)
- **Organization:** Global Business Travel Association (GBTA)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics & Conduct (chapter-level enforcement; Austin chapter as example)
- **URL (Austin chapter):** https://gbta-austinbta.org/Code_of_Ethics
- **Year:** Current (chapter-level implementation)
- **Key principles:** Good faith client relations (no intentional misrepresentation); respect buyer privacy and avoid soliciting during GBTA events; fair, ethical and legal trade practices; confidentiality of client transactions; cooperation with fellow members and promotion of professional growth. Violations may result in membership suspension or revocation.
- **Enforcement:** Chapter-level grievance process; membership suspension or revocation for violations.
- **Scope:** International/US (corporate travel management professionals)
- **Org type:** Professional/trade association
- **Note:** National GBTA code exists but is enforced primarily through chapters; Austin chapter document is the most publicly accessible version.

## Division 56 — Food and Beverage Service Activities

### ACF — Code of Professional Ethics (American Culinary Federation)
- **Organization:** American Culinary Federation (ACF)
- **Document:** Code of Professional Ethics
- **URL (PDF):** https://www.acfchefs.org/Downloads/Miscellaneous/Code-of-Professional-Ethics.pdf
- **Year:** Current (actively enforced; required for all full members and certified professionals)
- **Key principles:** Comply with all applicable laws and regulations promoting public health and safety; create work environments minimizing risk to colleagues' health and safety; support others' professional development and learning; non-discrimination in employment regardless of race, color, national origin, gender, religion, age, disability, political beliefs, sexual orientation, or marital/family status; do not misappropriate others' property for personal gain; do not make false statements about colleagues; accurately represent professional training and qualifications; do not aid misstatement of qualifications.
- **Enforcement:** Every full member and all ACF-certified individuals (CEC, CC, CPC, CCA, etc.) shall abide by this code; certification handbooks explicitly reference the code.
- **Scope:** US/International (professional cooks, chefs, culinary educators; 20,000+ members)
- **Org type:** Professional association

### NACE — Code of Ethics (National Association for Catering and Events)
- **Organization:** National Association for Catering and Events (NACE)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics
- **URL:** https://www.nace.net/ethics
- **Year:** Current (actively enforced with formal complaint process)
- **Key principles:** Take responsibility for conduct and advance NACE's mission, vision and values; foster open and welcoming environment respectful of all members regardless of race, color, religion, national origin, age, marital status, personal appearance, sexual orientation, gender identity, family responsibility, political affiliation, or disability; refrain from conduct or statements bringing discredit to NACE or the catering and events profession.
- **Enforcement:** Ethics complaints reviewed by an Ethics Committee using formal Ethics Review Procedures; Ethics Complaint Form submitted to ethics@nace.net or by mail.
- **Scope:** US (catering and event professionals)
- **Org type:** Professional association

### CMS-A — Code of Ethics and Professional Responsibility (Court of Master Sommeliers Americas)
- **Organization:** Court of Master Sommeliers Americas (CMS-A)
- **Document:** Code of Ethics and Professional Responsibility
- **URL:** https://www.mastersommeliers.org/about-us/code-of-conduct/
- **PDF:** https://www.mastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/CMS-A-Code-Ethics-and-Professional-Responsibility-May-2021r.pdf
- **Year:** May 2021 (most recent version)
- **Key principles:** Core values — Hospitality, Integrity, Humility, Self-Care, and Diversity — expected of all Master Sommeliers and candidates. Members must sign the Code of Ethics and complete training modules for diversity and harassment in the workplace. Whistleblower protection for those who report violations. Anonymous reporting available.
- **Enforcement:** Must sign Code of Ethics as eligibility condition for all CMS-A programming; retaliation protections for reporters.
- **Scope:** Americas (Master Sommeliers and sommelier candidates)
- **Org type:** Professional certification body / association

### USBG — Member Code of Conduct (United States Bartenders' Guild)
- **Organization:** United States Bartenders' Guild (USBG)
- **Document:** Member Code of Conduct
- **URL:** https://www.usbg.org/about1/usbg-policies/member-code-of-conduct2
- **Policies page:** https://www.usbg.org/about/usbg-policies
- **Year:** Current
- **Key principles:** Honesty, integrity and fairness in conduct; understand, support, promote and represent USBG Values, Vision, and Mission; respect dignity and rights regardless of race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, creed, gender identity, immigration status, mental and physical ability, sexual orientation, and veteran status; committed to welcoming, safe, rewarding and collaborative environment free from harassment, discrimination, intimidation and retaliation. Core values: Service, Integrity, Sharing Knowledge, Tradition, and Camaraderie.
- **Enforcement:** Misconduct investigation procedures; membership suspension and other remedial actions for violations.
- **Scope:** US (bartending professionals)
- **Org type:** Professional guild / association

### IFSEA — Professional Ethics Standards (International Food Service Executives Association)
- **Organization:** International Food Service Executives Association (IFSEA, est. 1901)
- **Document:** Professional ethics standards (embedded in certification and membership framework; no standalone public document confirmed)
- **URL:** https://ifsea.org/
- **Year:** Ongoing (organization est. 1901 — industry's oldest food service professional association)
- **Key principles:** Professional conduct and ethics are core membership and certification requirements. Examinees must sign Integrity Form acknowledging Examinee Code of Ethics and Cheating Policy. Certification programs (CFE, CFA, CFM) embed ethics requirements.
- **Enforcement:** Integrity Form required for all certification examinations; ethics embedded in certification standards.
- **Scope:** US/International (food service executives, military food service)
- **Org type:** Professional association
- **Note:** No standalone publicly available Code of Ethics document confirmed; ethics requirements embedded in certification framework. Included for historical completeness as oldest food service professional association. Lower confidence than ACF/NACE.
