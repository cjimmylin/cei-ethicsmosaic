## 7. Notable Findings

1. **Healthcare dominates**: NAICS 62 (Health Care and Social Assistance) has the richest ethics landscape, with 45 codes across ambulatory care (34), hospitals (4), nursing facilities (2), and social assistance (5). The AMA Code of Medical Ethics (1847) is among the oldest professional ethics codes in the world.

2. **Finance is the densest sector**: NAICS 52 (Finance and Insurance) has 58 codes, with Securities (523) alone contributing 21 codes. The CFA Institute Code is the gold standard, covering 190,000+ charterholders globally.

3. **Professional Services (541) is the single richest subsector**: With 44 codes spanning law (ABA), engineering (NSPE, ASCE, ASME, AIChE, IEEE), computing (ACM), psychology (APA), architecture (AIA), and more.

4. **Cross-industry codes are critical connectors**: Organizations like IEEE, NSPE, ACM, SAI (SA8000), ETI, amfori BSCI, FLA, and RBA appear across multiple sectors. These cross-cutting standards often provide the most comprehensive ethical frameworks.

5. **Age and continuity matter**: Many codes have century-plus lineages -- NAR (1913), NECA (1901), AMA (1847), APhA (1852), ASNE Canons of Journalism (1922), ICMA (1924). This institutional depth suggests deep-rooted ethical norms.

6. **Enforcement mechanisms vary widely**: Some codes are legally enforceable (ABA Model Rules, USPAP, FINRA), others are mandatory for certification (CFA, CFP, CPCU, ACM), and many are aspirational/voluntary (PRSA, SPJ, AMA Marketing).

7. **Supply chain ethics codes dominate manufacturing and retail**: Sectors 31-33 and 44-45 rely heavily on supply chain ethics standards (SA8000, ETI Base Code, FLA, WRAP, RBA, amfori BSCI) rather than point-of-production ethics.

8. **Public administration has layered regulatory ethics**: Sector 92 combines regulatory codes (5 CFR Part 2635), professional codes (ASPA, ICMA), and sector-specific standards (IACP for law enforcement, NDAA for prosecutors, NASA for space). Military ethics (Code of Conduct, JER) is uniquely codified by Executive Order.

9. **Gaps are meaningful**: Subsectors with zero codes (e.g., Machinery Manufacturing 333, Furniture Manufacturing 337, Pipeline Transportation 486, Scenic Transportation 487) represent potential blind spots in professional ethics coverage.

10. **Self-regulation traditions in specific industries**: The beverage alcohol industry (DISCUS since Prohibition repeal, Beer Institute since 1938, Wine Institute since 1949) and the toy industry (ICTI since 1995) show the longest-running voluntary self-regulatory traditions.
