This report synthesizes professional ethics codes identified across all 23 SOC major occupational groups (11-0000 through 55-0000). ## 7. Notable Findings

- **Healthcare dominates:** SOC 29-0000 (Healthcare Practitioners) has the densest ethics code coverage with 45 codes, reflecting the deep historical tradition of medical ethics dating to the Hippocratic Oath (ca. 400 BCE) and the AMA Code (1847).

- **Construction trades are well-covered by union codes:** Most major construction trade unions (IBEW, UA, SMART, Ironworkers, BAC, OPCMIA, UBC, Boilermakers, LIUNA, Roofers, IUPAT) have adopted codes of excellence or conduct, a pattern driven by labor-management partnerships to ensure craft quality and safety.

- **Legal profession has the longest self-regulation tradition:** ABA Model Rules (1983, replacing 1969 Code) serve as the template adopted by all 50 U.S. states. International coverage via IBA (2006), CCBE (1988), UN Basic Principles (1990), and Bangalore Principles (2002).

- **Financial occupations have the highest code density per minor group:** SOC 13-2000 (Financial Specialists) has 20 codes from major international bodies including CFA Institute, AICPA, IESBA, IIA, CFP Board, and actuarial societies.

- **Military ethics operates through a unique layered system:** Federal law (UCMJ), executive orders (Code of Conduct, EO 10631), DoD-wide regulation (JER/5500.07), service-specific values frameworks (LDRSHIP, Honor/Courage/Commitment), and individual creeds (Soldier's, Sailor's, Airman's).

- **Food service and building maintenance are the sparsest sectors:** SOC 35-0000 and 37-0000 each have only 5 codes. Most workers in these sectors operate under employer policies and union contracts rather than professional ethics codes.

- **Emerging pattern — ethics through certification:** In occupations lacking traditional professional associations, ethics codes are increasingly embedded in credentialing programs (ISA Certified Arborist, NCCCO crane operators, ACE personal trainers, NATE HVACR technicians).

- **Medical device and pharmaceutical sales codes have quasi-regulatory force:** AdvaMed and PhRMA codes are treated by U.S. enforcement authorities as compliance baselines for the Anti-Kickback Statute.

- **Cross-listed codes reveal professional overlap:** Organizations like ASSP, ISM, PMI, ISACA, NCRA, and AALL appear across multiple SOC groups, indicating shared ethical infrastructure across related occupations.

- **International codes complement national ones:** Most major professions have both a US/national code and an international counterpart (AMA/WMA, ANA/ICN, ABA/IBA, AIA/RIBA, AICPA/IESBA, NASW/IFSW, SPJ/IFJ).

- **NAR Code of Ethics (1913) is one of the oldest professional business codes**, predating most other industry codes and requiring mandatory triennial ethics training for over 1.5 million members.

- **Religious workers converged on a Common Code of Ethics in 2004** across six chaplaincy organizations, a rare instance of unified cross-denominational professional ethics in the US.

- **AI provisions are beginning to appear:** The ICF Code of Ethics (2025 revision) includes provisions on AI use in coaching — coaches must disclose use of artificial intelligence and ensure client interests are protected.
